Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B · Self-Forcing · NVIDIA A100-40GB

Real-time video diffusion on one GPU: where the time goes, and controlling when an object appears

Two controlled studies on the same model family. Part A prices every boundary a persistent megakernel would remove in a streaming video DiT and finds the one that matters is already fixed by split-KV. Part B replaces TempoControl's gradient-based timing control (2.6× time, ~110 GB) with a forward-only cross-attention bias costing about 1 % time on a 40 GB card, and carries it into real-time streaming generation where gradient methods cannot run.

Part A - Where does a real-time video DiT spend its time after CUDA graphs?

Self-Forcing runs Wan2.1-1.3B chunk by chunk: 30 blocks, 4,680 tokens per pass, five passes per chunk, batch size one. The megakernel literature promises up to 1.7× by removing kernel boundaries. We asked whether that transfers to this compute-bound regime on Ampere, by attribution through controlled intervention over 13 pre-registered jobs, every threshold frozen before its job.

Three boundaries, priced. Kernel-launch boundaries are worth 3.11 ms/pass after fusion - about 1 % of chunk time. An unanticipated RoPE-to-KV-cache materialization costs 19.7 ms/pass at 3–6 % of HBM bandwidth; fusing it recovered 10.7 ms/pass byte-identically. The dominant recoverable cost is a wave-quantization tail in FlashAttention-2: 19.2 % of attention time, because a 444-CTA grid on 108 SMs occupies 4.11 waves and launches five. Only that tail (9.5–12.1 % of chunk time) clears the pre-registered 5 % gate.

The verdict. An off-the-shelf arm, flash_attn_with_kvcache with an explicit num_splits=4, recovers the whole tail (19.5 % vs 19.2 %). The residual is not measurable against a 4 % build threshold, so the persistent, semaphore-scheduled kernel is not justified on this configuration. Left to its heuristic, FA2 picks about one split and recovers 3 %: the split count must be set explicitly.

One kernel built, one negative result. A Triton kernel fusing FA2's split-KV combine into the output-projection GEMM is numerically exact and 20 ms/pass slower: 255 registers per thread cap occupancy at one block per SM, the fp32 partials are re-read once per N-block (2.67 GB of L2 traffic vs 0.32 GB for the GEMM), and the tensor pipe is active 9 % of the time against 81 %. Removing the boundary was worth less than the kernel shape it forced.

Part B - Controlling when an object appears, without gradients

Asked for "a car appears during the second second", Wan2.1 usually shows the car from frame 0. TempoControl fixes this by gradient descent on the latent during sampling, at about 2.6× the time and ~110 GB of memory. We tested forward-only edits of the model's text cross-attention on TempoControl's own benchmark, scored with its official, unmodified metric.

The method (arm L). A per-frame bias on the object's text tokens in cross-attention: +β on latent frames where the object should be visible, −γ where it should not, β = γ = 2, conditional CFG branch only, all 30 blocks and 12 heads. No extra steps, no gradients, weights and prompt unchanged. A frame-independent boost of the timing words (arm U) changes nothing, as predicted: the text encoder has no notion of frames, so timing has to enter per frame.

Why it works and the alternatives fail. Padding tokens absorb ~92 % of cross-attention and the object token gets ~0.1 %, which sets the scale (β = 2 multiplies the object's odds by e² ≈ 7.4). L multiplies every query's odds by the same factor and keeps the spatial pattern; closed-form share targeting (arm S) forces every query of a frame to the same share, erases the spatial contrast, and the object never forms. β = γ = 4 destroys the video when the "off" window is the start.

Production path. The bias is rank-1 per frame, so it rides inside Wan's unmodified FlashAttention-2 call through 8 extra head dimensions (128 → 136): q carries the per-frame bias, the object keys carry constants summing to 1/scale, v is zero-padded. Zero table → bitwise equal to Wan's flash call. Cost +1.58 % time, +0.008 GB.

Streaming. Inside Self-Forcing the table is indexed by each chunk's frame offset and applied in every pass. The gain grows with lateness (+41/+44 pts for the last second). Against prompt switching at the onset chunk (Rolling-Forcing- and LongLive-style), L scores higher as measured (+4.8 pts, better CLIP and present frames) but renders substitute objects on 7 of 80 videos in a blinded count, against 0 for switching; with that correction the gap closes. It is a trade-off, not a dominance.

Honest costs. VBench imaging quality drops by 0.043 (CI excludes 0; pre-registered prediction missed). CLIP prompt similarity drops 0.01, consistently. The single-object Wan baseline (69.5 %) sits outside TempoControl's calibration band (63.9 ± 5), so only gains are compared there; on two objects the baselines agree (37.6 vs 37.5 %) and L reaches 47.2 % against their 53.2 %. The method is essentially TS-Attn specialised to one object: the contribution is the measurement.

What this is. Seed 42 for every video. B0 is the unmodified model; L adds +2 to the object's text tokens in cross-attention on frames where it should be visible and −2 where it should not, in the conditional branch only. For two objects, the static object gets +2 on every frame. No extra steps, no gradients, +0.9 % time.

Provenance. Single-object prompts dog (2nd), umbrella, skateboard and apple are verbatim benchmark prompts; their videos are the study's step-4 (Wan) and step-3b (Self-Forcing) runs. "Dog, last second" is not in the benchmark and the two-object prompts use the website's wording (the benchmark file words them differently), so those were generated for this page with the same protocol. Six prompts and one seed are illustrative only; the benchmark results (e.g. +15.5 points on all 80 single-object prompts, +9.6 on all 82 pairs) are in the study's findings.
